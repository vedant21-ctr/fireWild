Dual-Engine Decision Architecture

Bridging satellite raster dynamics with polyline-level tactical suppression operations.

Engine 1: Landscape Propagation LightGBM • 1 km Raster

Next Day Wildfire Spread (NDWS)

Predicts whether unburned landscape grid cells will transition into actively burning fire fronts within the next 24-hour cycle. Ingests 12 multi-spectral remote-sensing channels: SRTM elevation, GridMET daily atmospheric vectors (wind speed, direction, temperature, humidity, precipitation, ERC, PDSI), MODIS/VIIRS NDVI, human population density, and KDTree Euclidean distance to active fire.

Target: Spread transitions into unburned terrain (0.58%–0.96% prevalence)
Key Driver: Distance to flame front accounts for 70.5% of model split gain
Calibration: Optimal decision threshold $\tau^* = 0.0281$ maximizes $F_1$

Engine 2: Tactical Containment CLBI Pipeline • 100m Polylines

Candidate Line Breach Intelligence (CLBI)

Estimates the operational holding vs. burn-over probability for constructed and natural fireline segments (handlines, dozer lines, roads). Parameters incorporate acute fire attack angles, head-fire alignment, cleared barrier width, localized terrain slope, and upstream NDWS predicted fire intensity.

Tactical Action: Rank segments by vulnerability to guide dozer reinforcement
Risk Stratification: 4 operational tiers (Low, Medium, High, Critical)
Safety Advisory: Generates explainable risk drivers for incident commanders

Research & Methodology

Formal mathematical problem formulation, data schema, audit disclosures, and Phase 2 roadmap.

Peer-Audited Protocol CSAI Project 17

1. Problem Formulation & Target Semantics

Wildfire spread is a non-linear spatio-temporal phenomenon governed by fuel moisture, local topography, and atmospheric winds. In Project 17, we formulate Next Day Wildfire Spread (NDWS) as predicting 24-hour forward active spread transitions into previously unburned landscape cells:

$$Y_{\text{transition}}(r, c) = 1 \iff \left(\text{PrevFireMask}(r, c) = 0.0 \land \text{FireMask}(r, c) = 1.0\right)$$
Critical Target Distinction: Our local Phase 1 pipeline isolates true outward expansion. Already-burning core pixels ($\text{PrevFireMask} = 1.0$) are excluded from the positive target class. This contrasts with external baseline benchmarks (e.g. Huot et al., 2022 and PR #1) which predict all active fire ($Y = 1 \iff \text{FireMask} = 1.0$), conflating unextinguished core persistence with outward spread. Isolating true spread yields extreme class imbalance: 0.813% across the full dataset, 0.586% in validation (`train_14`), and 0.956% in held-out evaluation (`eval_00`).

2. Predictor Channels & Preprocessing

Each pixel sample contains 12 physical predictors observed strictly prior to spread (day $t$):

  • dist_to_prev_fire_km: Intra-patch Euclidean distance to active flame front via 2D spatial KDTree (sentinel 100.0 km if unignited).
  • th: Meteorological wind direction azimuth ($[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$ clockwise from North).
  • vs: 10m wind speed in meters per second (GridMET daily aggregate).
  • erc: National Fire Danger Rating System Energy Release Component (fuel dryness).
  • tmmn & tmmx: Daily minimum and maximum surface temperatures in Kelvin.
  • elevation: SRTM digital elevation in meters above sea level.
  • population: Gridded human settlement density (SEDAC GPWv4).
  • pdsi: Palmer Drought Severity Index (long-term soil moisture deficit).
  • sph: Specific humidity (atmospheric water vapor content).
  • NDVI: Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (vegetative greenness fraction).
  • pr: Daily accumulated precipitation in millimeters.

Strict Nodata Safeguard: All cells with $-1.0$ (cloud obscuration, water bodies, or sensor boundary nodata) are completely masked out using an explicit valid_mask prior to model training.

3. Audit Disclosures & Known Scope Gaps

In accordance with rigorous scientific pair-programming standards, the following implementation gaps and external dataset constraints are formally documented:

Gap A: Absence of 2D Focal Neighborhood Aggregations

While the tabular pipeline extracts an intra-patch Euclidean distance feature, it flattens pixels independently and does not compute 2D focal neighborhood aggregates (e.g. 3×3 or 5×5 focal mean of active fire pixels, localized terrain roughness, or focal NDVI). This limitation motivated the Phase 2 deep learning roadmap.

Gap B: Absence of Fixed False-Positive Rate Recall Sweeps

Evaluation metrics are reported at the validation-optimal $F_1$ decision threshold ($\tau^* = 0.028115$). Standard operational sweeps for recall at fixed false-positive rates (e.g. $\text{Recall}_{\text{FPR}=0.01}$ and $\text{Recall}_{\text{FPR}=0.05}$) were not implemented in the tabular metric function.

Finding C: Kaggle Elevation Tile Spatial Overlap

An independent terrain-fingerprinting audit revealed that 94.7% of official evaluation patches and 95.1% of test patches share identical 8×8 km SRTM elevation tiles with training data (split_audit.json). Models evaluated on standard Kaggle splits can memorize local elevation patterns; true out-of-region generalization drops from ~0.26 to ~0.138 PR-AUC on spatially disjoint holdouts.

4. Phase 2 Spatial Deep Learning Roadmap

Having established the audited tabular baseline ceiling with LightGBM (0.1145 Val AP), Phase 2 transitions to end-to-end spatial convolutional neural networks:

01
2D Multi-Scale Convolutional Architectures (U-Net / ConvNeXt): Ingesting full $64 \times 64 \times 12$ multi-channel raster tensors natively to capture 2D spatial texture and perimeter curvature.
02
Imbalance-Aware Spatial Loss Functions (Focal Tversky Loss): Directly optimizing boundary spatial overlap (Dice/IoU) under severe class imbalance without relying on pixel-wise cross-entropy.
03
Directional Convective Wind Feature Synthesis: Formulating explicit directional dot-product channels between 2D terrain elevation gradients ($\nabla z$) and atmospheric wind vectors ($\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}}$).
04
Ecoregion-Disjoint Spatial Partitioning: Partitioning fire incidents by USFS Ecoregion provinces to eliminate the 94.7% terrain fingerprinting leakage discovered in the Kaggle release.
